AI conversation coach for nursing communication training

1. Introduction

1.1 Background

Nurses face a range of workplace challenges, including high workloads, work-related stress and burnout. A meta-analysis conducted by Ge et al. (2023), involving 94 studies across more than 30 countries, estimated the global prevalence of nursing burnout at 30%. Similarly, a study of 1,826 nurses at Singapore General Hospital found that 39% experienced high emotional exhaustion, 40% high depersonalisation, and 59% low personal accomplishment (Ang, 2016). Furthermore, Kiptulon (2024) identified several factors contributing to work-related stress among nurses, including understaffing, role conflict, bullying, and a lack of support from supervisors and colleagues. Their systematic review also found that positive organisational cultures, characterised by effective communication, teamwork, and supportive leadership, were associated with lower levels of work-related stress. These findings highlight the importance of providing nurses with appropriate guidance and a supportive working environment to help them manage workplace challenges and maintain their well-being.

Effective communication is essential to nursing practice, enabling nurse managers to support staff development, address workplace challenges, and foster a positive work environment (Meneses-La-Riva, 2025). Nurse managers also play an important role in guiding staff nurses through challenges such as workplace conflicts, work-related stress and professional development. Therefore, having effective coaching skills can help nurse managers support their staff in handling these situations. Unlike traditional directive leadership approaches, coaching leadership emphasises active listening, reflective questioning, and helping individuals develop their own solutions. By adopting a coaching approach, nurse managers can encourage staff nurses to reflect on their experiences and identify areas for improvement, while also helping them build confidence, improve communication, and take greater ownership of their professional development (Richardson, 2023). To promote a coaching-oriented leadership culture, the National University Hospital (NUH) incorporates coaching principles into its Exemplary Leader Coaching Programme (ELCP) through a one-day masterclass. In the masterclass, nurse managers learn coaching mindsets, communication techniques, the 3P and the GROW-L frameworks. The 3P framework consists of Partner, Practice and Process. The GROW-L framework guides coaching conversations through five stages, which are Goal, Reality, Options, Will, and Learning.

While the ELCP provides nurse managers with a foundation in coaching principles, they still need repeated practice, reflection, and feedback to develop their coaching and communication skills. Applying these techniques in the workplace can be challenging, especially when nurse managers face sensitive conversations or staff nurses who resist feedback. Nurse managers who are used to giving direct instruction may also find it difficult to change their existing communication habits. Therefore, repeated coaching practice can help nurse managers become more familiar with techniques they have learnt and apply them more effectively in real workplace conversations.

1.2 Problem statement

Nurse managers who undergo NUH's ELCP are taught structured coaching principles and skills through the one-day masterclass and are expected to apply these skills in workplace coaching conversations. However, they may have limited opportunities to practice coaching conversations before applying these skills with actual staff nurses. They may also have limited opportunities to receive immediate feedback on their coaching approach. As a result, nurse managers may find it challenging to apply the coaching techniques they have learnt in real workplace conversations.

1.3 Value proposition

The proposed AI-based coaching simulator aims to provide nurse managers with an on-demand platform to practice coaching conversations based on the principles introduced during the ELCP masterclass. Through the coaching simulator, nurse managers will take on the role of the coach and interact with an AI staff nurse who acts as the coachee. The AI staff nurse will respond to the nurse manager based on the different coaching scenarios, allowing nurse managers to handle different workplace situations. After each conversation, the system will provide structured feedback based on the 3P and GROW-L frameworks to help nurse managers identify their strengths and areas for improvement. The system also allows nurse managers to retry scenarios and try different responses at their own pace. Through repeated practice and feedback, the simulator helps nurse managers to build confidence before applying the techniques they have learnt in real workplace conversations

1.4 Objectives and Scope

The main objective of this project is to develop an AI-based coaching simulator that allows nurse managers to practice coaching conversations with virtual staff nurses across different scenarios created.

The specific objectives of this project are:

  • To develop an AI-based conversation system that allows nurse managers to interact with AI staff nurse in different coaching scenarios
  • To develop an AI-based assessment system based on 3P and GROW-L frameworks to evaluate the nurse managers’ coaching skills and provide structured feedback on areas demonstrated well and on areas that can be improved
  • To develop both web- based and virtual reality (VR) interfaces that allow nurse managers to practice coaching conversations
  • To incorporate emotional responses and facial expressions into the virtual nurse staff to make coaching more realistic

This project focuses on coaching conversations between a nurse manager who acts as a coach and an AI staff nurse who acts as the coachee. The simulator is a coaching tool that will only assess the nurse manager’s coaching skills, while the AI staff nurse will respond based on the conversation. The simulator is not meant to replace the NUH’s existing ELCP masterclass. It is for nurse managers to practice the coaching techniques they learnt in the masterclass and to build confidence before applying them in real workplace conversations.



2. Background Research

2.1 Primary research

To gain a deeper insight into the problem, regular meetings were held with the nurse head at NUH from September 2026 onwards. The purpose was to understand how coaching skills are trained and assessed in the hospitals, and what practice tools are needed to assist them with improving the coaching skills of nurse managers. The project initially explored AI-supported practice for nursing communication in general, but these meetings helped narrow its focus to coaching conversations for nurse managers. Therefore, the meetings allowed the team to clarify requirements and keep the design aligned with NUH’s expectations as the project progressed.

Through these meetings, several challenges with coaching training were raised by the nurse head. Firstly, it was mentioned that the ELCP masterclass alone is not sufficient for nurse managers to build their confidence in coaching, as traditional role play, which is the common method to practise, requires facilitators and time, which limits the opportunities for nurse managers to practise. Furthermore, the coaching experience and feedback received may vary depending on the facilitator and training setting. As nurse managers encounter increasingly complex workplace interactions, such as emotionally sensitive conversations and staff-related challenges, opportunities to practise realistic coaching scenarios become particularly important. These findings suggest a need for a more accessible and reliable practice platform that allows nurse managers to reinforce their coaching skills beyond the masterclass.
